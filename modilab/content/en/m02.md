### Learning objectives
After completing this module you should be able to:
1. write Newton's equations of motion for a system of atoms,
2. explain why these equations must be solved numerically,
3. compare the stability of the Euler and velocity Verlet integrators.

### Concepts
Every atom moves according to Newton's second law, $m\,\ddot{\mathbf r}_i = \mathbf F_i$, where $\mathbf F_i$ is the sum of the forces from all other atoms. For two atoms the problem reduces to a single relative coordinate $r$ with reduced mass $\mu = m/2$. For hundreds of atoms there is no analytical solution, so the computer must step forward in time with a small step $\Delta t$.

The simplest approach is the **explicit Euler method**:

$$r(t+\Delta t) = r(t) + \Delta t\,v(t), \qquad v(t+\Delta t) = v(t) + \Delta t\,a(t)$$

It looks reasonable, but it has a serious weakness: the total energy keeps growing from step to step.

**Velocity Verlet** updates the velocity in two symmetric half steps:

$$v(t+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}) = v(t) + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}a(t), \quad r(t+\Delta t) = r(t) + \Delta t\,v(t+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}), \quad v(t+\Delta t) = v(t+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}) + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}a(t+\Delta t)$$

This algorithm is *time reversible* and *symplectic*, so its energy only oscillates around the correct value without drifting. That is why velocity Verlet is the standard integrator in almost all MD software.

### Analysis questions
1. At $\Delta t^* = 0.01$, how many times larger is the energy deviation of Euler than that of velocity Verlet?
2. Halve $\Delta t^*$. By how much does the energy deviation of velocity Verlet decrease? Is this consistent with a second order error ($\propto \Delta t^2$)?
3. In phase space the true trajectory should be a closed curve. Explain the physical meaning of the Euler trajectory spiralling outwards.
