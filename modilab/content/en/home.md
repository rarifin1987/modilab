MoDiLab is a virtual laboratory for learning **molecular dynamics (MD)**, the method that computes the motion of every atom from Newton's laws and derives the macroscopic properties of matter from that motion. All simulations here use the Lennard-Jones potential, the simplest model that still captures the essential physics: atoms attract each other at moderate distances and repel when pushed too close.

**How to learn with this application.** Work through the modules in order. Each module contains learning objectives, an explanation of the concepts, a simulation you can control yourself, analysis questions and a short quiz. Do not just press buttons: before running a simulation, write down what you expect to happen, then compare.

**Module map.** Modules 1 and 2 deal with one and two atoms: interatomic forces and how a computer solves the equations of motion. Module 3 explains how a few hundred atoms can represent matter containing around 10²³ atoms. Modules 4 and 5 connect atomic motion with temperature and statistics. Module 6 shows how the structure of gases, liquids and solids can be recognised from atomic positions. The free laboratory is available for exploration beyond the modules.

**Units.** In the left panel you can choose the display units: *reduced* (σ, ε, m, valid for every Lennard-Jones atom) or *real* (K, ps, Å, meV, m/s and so on) based on the reference element you select. This setting applies to all modules and the laboratory. Simulation inputs are still given in reduced units, and their equivalents are shown below them. Get used to switching between the two systems: MD researchers work in reduced units but report results in real units.
