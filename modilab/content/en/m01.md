### Learning objectives
After completing this module you should be able to:
1. explain the origin of attractive and repulsive forces between atoms,
2. interpret the physical meaning of the parameters σ and ε in the Lennard-Jones potential,
3. calculate the equilibrium distance and binding energy of a pair of atoms.

### Concepts
Two neutral atoms far apart hardly affect each other. When they approach, the electron cloud of each atom fluctuates and induces momentary dipoles in the other, giving rise to a weak attraction called the van der Waals or London dispersion force. This attraction weakens in proportion to $r^{-6}$. When the atoms are pushed closer still, their electron clouds begin to overlap and the Pauli exclusion principle produces a very strong repulsion.

The Lennard-Jones potential combines both effects:

$$U(r) = 4\varepsilon\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right]$$

The $r^{-6}$ term has a sound theoretical basis. The $r^{-12}$ term is chosen mainly because it is easy to compute, not because it follows from theory. The parameter **σ** is the distance at which $U = 0$ and can be thought of as the "diameter" of the atom. The parameter **ε** is the depth of the potential well, the energy required to separate two atoms from their equilibrium position.

The force is the negative derivative of the energy with respect to distance:

$$F(r) = -\frac{dU}{dr} = \frac{24\varepsilon}{r}\left[2\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right]$$

The force vanishes exactly at the bottom of the well, at $r_{min} = 2^{1/6}\sigma \approx 1.122\,\sigma$. This is where two atoms would rest if they had no kinetic energy.
