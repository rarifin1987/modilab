### Learning objectives
After completing this module you should be able to:
1. write the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution in two and three dimensions,
2. explain how collisions drive a system towards thermal equilibrium,
3. use the speed distribution as a test of equilibrium in a simulation.

### Concepts
In thermal equilibrium, the probability that an atom has energy $E$ is proportional to the Boltzmann factor $e^{-E/k_BT}$. For kinetic energy this leads to the **Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution**:

$$f_{2D}(v) = \frac{mv}{k_BT}\,e^{-mv^2/2k_BT}, \qquad f_{3D}(v) = 4\pi v^2\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2} e^{-mv^2/2k_BT}$$

The factors $v$ (2D) and $v^2$ (3D) come from the number of velocity directions that give the same speed. This is why the probability of zero speed is zero, even though a zero velocity component is the most likely.
